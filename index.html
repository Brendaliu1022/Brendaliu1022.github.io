Research

Covariance steering for safe spacecraft rendezvous

Advisor: Yuri Shimane, Assistant Professor of Mechanical and Aerospace Engineering, UC Irvine, eXploration Logistics and Control (XLC) group. September 2025 to present.

When two spacecraft meet in orbit, navigation is imperfect and thrusters are noisy, and a small error at a few meters per second can end in a collision. We design the nominal trajectory and the spread of possible errors at the same time, so that safety constraints hold with a chosen probability rather than only in the ideal case.

  1. Relative motion in quasi-nonsingular relative orbit elements, with J₂ and differential drag in an augmented 9-state model
  2. Output feedback through an offline-precomputed Kalman filter
  3. Chance constraint on control magnitude, recast as a second-order cone through a χ² quantile bound
  4. Sequential convex programming with a linearized e/i-vector passive-safety constraint
  5. Sparse, hands-off feedback gains through iteratively reweighted ℓ1,p regularization
  6. Closed-loop validation by nonlinear Monte Carlo simulation

My part

I wrote most of the code for this project, across the group’s two Julia packages: SpacecraftRendezVous.jl for dynamics and simulation, and the covariance-control solver.

  • Implemented the QNS-ROE dynamics with J₂ and differential drag, including the augmented drag model and its empirical calibration, with unit tests and continuous integration.
  • Formulated and solved the covariance-steering problem in JuMP with the Clarabel solver: Kalman-filter output feedback, second-order-cone chance constraints, and the SCP loop with passive safety.
  • Implemented sparse feedback gains (IRL1P, after Kumagai and Oguri) and traced the cost–sparsity trade-off: removing about half of the active feedback steps costs less than 5% in transient covariance.
  • Built the nonlinear Monte Carlo pipeline: 200 closed-loop runs met the 1% chance constraint with no violations.
  • Presented the work at an AIAA conference in April 2026 and am writing the paper as first author with Prof. Shimane.

Servo drive electronics and motor-control firmware

Engineering intern, Hunan Yanggao CNC Equipment Co., Ltd., summer 2025

I worked on a two-board servo drive for permanent-magnet motors. The control board carries a TI TMS320F28335 DSP with encoder, SPI, serial and JTAG interfaces. It stacks on a three-phase inverter board built around an intelligent power module, with a rectifier bridge, 450 V DC-bus capacitors, Hall-effect current sensing and optocoupler isolation, and accepts 20–220 V AC or 20–380 V DC.

  • Helped manufacture the control and driver boards from the company’s Altium Designer layouts and bill of materials.
  • Configured the field-oriented-control firmware in Code Composer Studio for a new motor: pole pairs, encoder resolution, rated current and initial rotor alignment.
  • Tested the motor drive on the bench with an oscilloscope: SVPWM at 10 kHz with nested current and speed loops, plus encoder and sliding-mode-observer speed estimation.

Microcomputer control of electromechanical systems

Princeton University online research program, Prof. Michael Littman, February to June 2025

I simulated a soft conductive sensor with eight boundary electrodes in COMSOL and scripted the solves and data export from MATLAB. Cycling the current injection around the electrodes gives each deformation a 64-value voltage signature, and bending and elongation leave clearly different patterns.

I also designed op-amp PID and PD controllers for a motor-angle loop, simulated them in Tinkercad and Falstad, and tuned the gains with the Ziegler–Nichols method in MATLAB.
